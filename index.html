    Storefront Copilot

    An agent that audits a creator’s storefront and proposes revenue-driving changes — with a human-in-the-loop review flow merchants actually trust.

    +14% GMV for merchants who approved ≥1 suggestion

    AI × Monetization · Lead designer · 2025

    Storefront Copilot

    The problem

    Small merchants knew their storefronts underperformed but didn’t know why. An earlier “AI tips” panel had a 4% engagement rate: generic advice, no way to act on it.

    The approach

    • Reframed the feature from “advice” to proposed diffs: every suggestion is a concrete, previewable, reversible change.
    • Designed confidence states for probabilistic output: estimated impact shown as a range, never a promise.
    • Masked agent latency with progressive results: the first fix appears in under 2 seconds while the deeper audit streams in.
    • Built a one-tap undo and a 7-day “what changed” log so merchants stay in control.

    The outcome

    64% of suggestions were accepted; merchants who approved at least one saw a 14% GMV lift over 60 days. Support tickets about “the AI changed my store” stayed at zero, because it never changes anything without approval.

    Payouts, Unpaused

    The problem

    Creators waited up to 7 days for earnings. Risk reviews were silent, so a delayed payout looked like a broken product.

    The approach

    • Mapped every payout state, including the uncomfortable ones: held, under review, reversed.
    • Replaced vague errors with plain-language status and a clear next step for each state.
    • Priced the instant option transparently: the fee is shown before the tap, not after.

    The outcome

    41% fewer payout support tickets, and 22% of eligible creators adopted instant payouts in the first quarter.

    The problem

    The approach

    • Designed a guided builder with three starting templates and editable perks.
    • Showed recommended price bands with the “why” beside them, so the creator keeps final say.

    AI · Product designer · 2025

    The Price Is (Probably) Right

    The problem

    A single-number price suggestion was ignored 80% of the time. Merchants didn’t trust a number they couldn’t question.

    The approach

    • Swapped the point estimate for a range with a plain-language rationale.
    • Designed low-confidence and no-data states so the model can say “I don’t know” gracefully.
    • Let merchants set guardrails (floor, ceiling, never discount) the model must respect.

    The outcome

    Suggestion adoption tripled, and suggested listings sold through 9% faster.

N° 03 — Approach

How I work

  1. 01 Find the money moment

    Every flow has one step where value changes hands. I find it in the data first, then design around it.

  2. 02 Design every state

    Empty, loading, streaming, partial, wrong, offline, and the 200-character product name. AI multiplies the number of states, so I map them before I make anything pretty.

  3. 03 Prototype the behavior

    Static mockups can’t show latency or uncertainty. I prototype with real model output so we test how it feels, not just how it looks.

  4. 04 Ship, measure, earn trust

    Feature flags, clear success metrics, and guardrail metrics for trust, like refund rate, undo rate and support tickets.

N° 04 — For the engineers

A designer who reads the diff

Handoff isn’t a wall I throw things over. There is no wall. There’s a Figma link, and every layer in it has a name.

The handoff contract

  • Design tokens that map 1:1 to code. No “Blue-ish 3”.
  • Components named for what they are: CheckoutSheet, not Frame 88.
  • Edge cases shipped with the spec, not discovered in QA.
  • WCAG 2.2 AA from the first frame: contrast, focus order, target sizes.
  • I know what a race condition is, and I’ll design the loading state for it.
